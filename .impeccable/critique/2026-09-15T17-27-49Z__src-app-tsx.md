# Design critique — Midnight Studio

Target: `src/App.tsx` and its browse, playback, About, and recovery states. Mode: Experience.

## Design specificity verdict

**The studio feels authored and worth exploring.** Matte equipment, colored cassette labels, restrained lighting, and the actual cassette-to-deck transition give it an identity specific to this portfolio. The contemporary execution and retro objects work together.

The surrounding introduction is more interchangeable. Its large headline and generous spacing delay the distinctive artifact and its instructions, especially on short laptops. The biggest opportunity is making the studio easier to enter and its content easier to read.

**Detector evidence:** The source scan found 33 advisories: 32 font-size and one radius. Thirty-one font-size locations are in `src/components/Stage.module.css`; the other is the documented mobile title at `src/components/CRT.module.css:439`. The radius at `CRT.module.css:26` is overridden by the active embedded screen. Browser scans produced 85 repeated rule instances across four views, including 55 small-text flags. Many flags concern intentional CRT glow, scanlines, silkscreen tracking, or documented typography. The useful additional finding is miniature text carrying functional guidance and content disclosures.

## Overall impression

The object design is the strongest part of the site. The weaker part is the handoff from admiring the studio to selecting a tape and reading it. Preserve the visual world and improve those transitions.

## What’s working

- **A coherent material language.** Broad planes, selective bevels, matte graphite, and the blue CRT make the scene tactile and contemporary.
- **A meaningful physical interaction.** Tape selection changes the scene, and Eject restores focus to the selected archive link. Reduced motion skips the sequence; the About content remained available without WebGL.
- **Strong phone playback.** At 390×844, 16px prose and a nearly full-height reader preserve the CRT character while giving content room.

## Priority issues

### 1. [P1] Keyboard users lose access to the reading area after tabbing away

Initial PageDown and arrow-key scrolling work. After Tab reaches transport on Alpha, focus cycles between Sound and Eject; the article has no stop in that cycle. Someone adjusting sound cannot tab back to continue reading.

**Fix:** Give the scrollable article a named, visibly focused keyboard stop and include it in the playback focus cycle. Preserve the heading announcement on entry. Sources: `src/components/CRT.tsx:40`, `src/components/Stage.tsx:137`.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable harden`

### 2. [P2] The first viewport makes the studio harder to use than to admire

At 1366×768, the canvas starts at y=352 and extends to y=953; the selection guidance and tape index sit below the first viewport. At 390×844, the studio becomes roughly 280×180px, making individual cassettes tiny. Both assessments independently found this handoff weak.

**Fix:** Reduce introductory vertical space on short laptops, frame the screen and tapes closer on phones, and put a readable selection hint beside the artifact. Retain the accessible HTML index. Sources: `src/components/Stage.module.css:56`, `src/components/Stage.module.css:103`, `src/components/Stage.module.css:505`, `src/components/studio/StudioScene.tsx:59`.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable layout`

### 3. [P2] A wider viewport can produce a worse reader

At 600px wide, the projected reader is approximately 467×350px and its prose appears at 14.2px. The 390px phone reader offers roughly 694px of height and 16px prose. Crossing the 599px breakpoint sharply reduces reading comfort.

**Fix:** Use the full-height reader whenever the projected CRT cannot provide sufficient text size and reading height. A wider reader breakpoint is a practical starting point. Sources: `src/components/Stage.tsx:51`, `src/components/studio/StudioScene.tsx:59`, `src/components/CRT.module.css:422`.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable adapt`

### 4. [P2] Useful instructions inherit miniature hardware typography

HTML placeholder labels are 7px; phone selection guidance and curation disclosure are 8px; contact links are 9px. These communicate how to use the site and what is real. Their importance exceeds their visual weight. This is the detector’s strongest additional contribution.

**Fix:** Establish a readable floor for functional captions, instructions, and links—approximately 12–14px to start—while retaining small decorative serial numbers. Sources: `src/components/Stage.module.css:275`, `src/components/Stage.module.css:529`, `src/components/Stage.module.css:551`, `src/components/Stage.module.css:565`.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable typeset`

### 5. [P2] Action language leaves the visitor guessing what to press

“Pick something. Press play.” implies separate selection and playback, although one click opens a tape. Internal tape and About links also use the same ↗ symbol as external contact links.

**Fix:** Say “Choose a tape to play” at the interaction point, use a play symbol for playback, and reserve ↗ for external destinations. Sources: `src/components/Stage.tsx:312`, `src/components/Stage.tsx:479`, `src/components/studio/StudioScene.tsx:145`.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify`

## Cognitive load and emotional journey

The checklist finds two failures: six equal choices exceed its four-item threshold for both chunking and visible options. This is moderate by the rubric, but six clearly labeled tapes are reasonable for a gallery. Keep them visible; make a purposeful first play easier to recognize. Grouping, single focus, working-memory support, and progressive disclosure otherwise work.

The emotional peak is discovering the studio and inserting a tape. The valley is reaching placeholder prose, compounded by reader friction. Placeholder content is honest and already scheduled for Stage 8. About currently gives the clearest explanation that the machine itself is the work; making that interpretation easier to discover would strengthen the visit’s ending.

## Persona red flags

- **Jordan, first-time visitor:** The short-laptop viewport hides selection guidance; “Press play” suggests searching for a deck control.
- **Sam, keyboard user:** Initial reading works, but tabbing to Sound removes the ordinary keyboard route back into the article.
- **Casey, phone visitor:** The HTML index is easy to tap; miniature physical tapes and 8px guidance make the initial scene less approachable.

## Minor observations

- A missing tape says “THIS TAPE DOES NOT EXIST” in the CRT but “Channel not found” in transport (`Stage.tsx:527`).
- “About Daniel” loses “About” in its accessible name (`Stage.tsx:444`).
- Phone reset and sound controls have 32×32px hit areas; enlarge the hit areas while retaining their visual size (`Stage.module.css:539`).
- The focused NO SIGNAL heading has a rectangular outline that resembles a button; use the established screen-heading focus treatment (`CRT.module.css:168`).

## Questions to consider

- Could About become the intentional first play while the studio itself is the main portfolio piece?
- How much of the opening viewport should belong to the artifact and its selectable tapes?
- At what projected screen size does showing the full monitor stop helping the reader?
